Divina MIDI

Dante's Divina Commedia turned into music in three ways, each a step closer to music that means something. Every word of the poem becomes a note, so a canto lasts about as long as reading it aloud.

The emotional arc

The sentiment level relies on two Italian classifiers reading each tercet. Their scores follow the arc of the poem: the Inferno is the darkest cantica, the Paradiso the brightest. Canto IV of the Inferno, the Limbo of the great poets of antiquity, is its least painful canto.

How the sentiment becomes music

CanticaRegister: the Inferno low, the Paradiso high
TercetVerses end on the fifth, the third, then the root: open, open, closed

Scores are smoothed over neighbouring tercets, so the music follows the emotional arc rather than reacting to every line. The models have limits worth knowing: they read words, not irony, and they find Dante's language gloomy overall, which is why only the relative position of a tercet within the poem is used. The feasibility notebook shows where they succeed and where they fail.
